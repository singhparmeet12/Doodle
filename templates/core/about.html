📖 The Origin Story

How A Crooked Scribble Became A Universe

A small manifesto on why the world needs more uneven margins, melted crayons, and unapologetic daydreaming.

"It started on a Tuesday when the world was being far too orderly."

In an era of ultra-clean minimalist websites, uniform 8-pixel geometric grids, and pristine sans-serif typography that looks like it was generated by a sterile laboratory robot, our hands began to ache for something tactile. Something that looked like it had been drawn with a slightly dull 2B pencil on the back of a grocery receipt.

"Perfection is boring. A crooked smile on a doodle blob has ten times more heart than a pristine vector icon."

That was the afternoon Barnaby made his first appearance in the bottom right-hand corner of a college chemistry notebook. He wasn't planned. He didn't have a corporate pitch deck or a multi-tiered marketing funnel. He was just a curious little blob of yellow wax with two dot eyes, staring out at the classroom like he wanted to go outside and climb a birch tree.

Soon after, Pip the Pencil Bird landed next to him, complaining loudly about the quality of classroom sharpeners. Then Sir Reginald Cloud floated down from the ceiling wearing an immaculate top hat and monocle. Before we knew it, the margin had expanded, swallowed the textbook, and become Scribbleverse.

🖍️ The Scribbleverse Manifesto

1. Never Use A Ruler

If a line wobbles, that’s just your heartbeat showing up in the drawing.

2. Embrace Mistakes

An accidental coffee spill is just the start of a very dramatic monster storm.

3. Color Outside Lines

The lines were put there by people who forgot how fun sidewalks are.

4. Keep Soft Pencils Close

A dark, buttery stroke gives you permission to be brave on paper.
